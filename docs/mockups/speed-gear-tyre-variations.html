Speed cluster: gear, speed and tyre

Three replacements for the m/s speed readout, after the F1 game's wheel display: a gear for fun, speed in km/h, and a tyre gauge in place of the battery that holds at 100% until tyres wear.

The frames run a scripted lap: grid start, a straight, a braking zone, a mini-turbo, a boost pad past top speed (rev limiter), then a reverse. Grab the speed slider to take over.

A. Twin pods closest to the reference

Bottom centre. Left pod: tyre ring and percentage over a tyre icon. Centre: the gear, with five shift lights under it. Right pod: rev arc around km/h and RPM. The pods are the one translucent backing in the panel, so they read over the road.

B. Rev strip 16-bit, no box (ADR 0023)

Bottom left, opposite the course map. A row of chunky shift lights (green, red, blue, as on an F1 wheel), the gear in a bevelled box that matches the item slot, km/h beside it, and a top-down kart with four tyres under it. The four tyres share one value today; they can split per corner later.

C. Steering wheel screen the F1 cockpit look

Bottom centre, a pixel steering wheel peeking up from the frame edge with its screen: shift lights across the top, tyre left, gear centre, km/h and RPM right. The most character, and the most screen it covers.

How the gear and RPM are faked

Six gears split the sim's 30 m/s top speed (108 km/h) into 18 km/h bands, so gear 1 runs to 5 m/s. RPM climbs from 4,000 to 11,500 across each band and drops on the upshift. Past top speed (mini-turbo 140, boost pad 151 km/h) the kart stays in 6th on the limiter and the lights flash. Standing still reads N; reversing reads R. None of this touches the sim: it is a pure function of the speed the client already shows.
