1. Timing tower one plate, one edge

The take: the stack is one object, a broadcast timing tower. The position is a livery band set into the top of a single carbon plate and the rows under it share a label column, so TIME and BEST line up and the plate has one straight right edge. Trades: the tower is wider than today's lap plate and covers more sky; the position loses its own drop and reads a little less like a badge.

2. Only the position earns a plate outlined type over the View

The take: plates are for the things you glance at as objects: your place, your item, your gear. The clocks are type, white with a one-pixel ink outline and the drop, labels over values like the speed readout, TIME and BEST side by side. Trades: the most View shows through and the block is short, leaving room under the item slot; outlined type over a bright sky is the thing to check in the real game, and the stack reads less as a set.

3. Pit board digit tiles on a slab

The take: the crew holds your numbers out over the wall. One near-black slab, each character on its own white tile in ink, the position's tiles in the livery as the only colour, a best not yet set on carbon tiles, and a short pole under it. Trades: the most in-world of the three and the tiles keep every clock's width by construction; it is also the heaviest block, and ink on white inverts the panel's white-on-dark rule everywhere but the position.
