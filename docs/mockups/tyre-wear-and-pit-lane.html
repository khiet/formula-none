Proposal - 2026-11-03 - decisions taken

Tyre wear and the pit lane

Tyres wear with how a kart is driven, the kart gets slower and harder to hold as they go, and a real pit lane on the three big courses puts on a new set in a stop whose length is down to chance.

Recommendation

  • One tyre life, 100% to 0%, in the gauge already bottom-left of the Instrument panel. Cornering load and drifting do most of the wearing; hits, kerbs, walls, grass and braking do the rest.
  • Gentle, then a cliff. Little is lost above 60%. Below 30%, where the gauge turns red, grip and speed fall away quickly. At 0% the kart still reaches 93 km/h and can always finish.
  • A driven pit lane on Bell Deer, Goldstone and Monarch, limited to 54 km/h, with eight pit boxes in front of the existing pit building and a crew in each.
  • A stop lasts 1.9 s to 12 s by chance, median 2.8 s. The whole visit costs about 8 s, or a sixth of a lap.
  • Bots wear and pit too, and each bot level makes the call differently.

The one finding that changes the design

Three laps is too short for a stop to be the normal plan. In the model, a driver who manages their tyres and never stops beats a driver who pushes and stops by 2.4 to 3.9 s on every course with a pit lane. At five laps it flips: one stop beats no stop by 8 to 14 s. So at three laps the stop rescues a kart that was hit or overdriven, and strategy proper needs a five-lap race. See the numbers.

Decisions taken

Every recommendation was accepted on 2026-11-03 and is marked Chosen. They are recorded in ADRs 0080 to 0083.

  1. How long are races?
    1. Keep 3 laps as Sprint and add a 5-lap Grand Prix the creator can pick (Chosen)
    2. Always 3 laps: the stop stays a rescue
    3. Always 5 laps: every race has strategy, and takes about 4.5 minutes rather than 2.6
  2. Is the stop's length pure chance?
    1. Pure chance, as you asked; the driver only waits for green (Chosen)
    2. Chance, plus a small launch bonus for reacting to green, like the rocket start
  3. Who works the pit limiter?
    1. It engages by itself at the entry line, so no new control and no penalty (Chosen)
    2. A limiter key, with F1's 5 s penalty for speeding: truer, but one more key and an easy way to lose a race by accident
  4. Does the oval get a pit lane?
    1. No. A 16 s lap makes a stop cost a third of a lap, and tyres last the race anyway (Chosen)
    2. Yes, for consistency, though it would rarely be used
  5. Can a room turn tyres off?
    1. Yes: the creator picks Tyres On or Off, fixed for the room like the bot level, On by default (Chosen)
    2. No: tyres are always part of the game

What happens in F1, and what we take

Wear and degradation differ. Sliding overheats the surface and costs grip until the driver eases off; enough heat changes the tread's chemistry for good.1 Worn rubber also just runs out.One irreversible life for all of it in v1. Reversible heat is the first thing to add later.
Sliding feeds itself: hotter means less grip, less grip means more sliding.2Drifting is the fastest steady wear. Mini-turbos remain worth it, at a price.
Load matters. Some tracks punish the fronts through fast corners, others the rears through traction.3 Wheelspin overheats the rears.4Wear grows with the square of cornering load. Each mini-turbo release pays a small wheelspin cost. The four tyres share one value, as ADR 0031 placed them.
Hard braking locks a wheel and leaves a flat spot, with vibration and sometimes a forced stop.5A spin-out takes a lump of life, as if the kart flat-spotted all four. Hard braking wears a little.
Kerbs and debris damage tyres.6 Worn tread is more exposed: Vettel's failed tyre at Spa 2015 had about 30% tread left.7Kerb launches, tyre walls, walls and kart contact all cost life. No random punctures: a kart that loses a race should see why.
Loss is often steady, around 0.2 to 0.3 s a lap in one report,2 and can then drop sharply. The "cliff" is the name for that, though Pirelli has moved away from building one in.8Two gentle bands and a cliff at 30%, because a short race needs a warning players can read. This is deliberately more dramatic than current F1.

The pit lane

In F1In Formula None
The limit is 80 km/h from entry line to exit line, 60 at tight venues such as Monaco. Going over costs 5 s in a race, and five of Monaco 2026's six offenders did 60.1.954 km/h from line to line, held automatically. A limit in proportion to F1's would be 27 km/h, which feels broken; 54 keeps the visit's cost right instead.
A visit costs 20 s or more: 21 s at Monaco in one analysis, 20 to 25 s in an F1 25 strategy guide.10 That is about a quarter of a lap on a 90 s lap (my arithmetic).About 8 s, a sixth of a lap. Cheaper than F1 because the race is so short.
A crew of about 23 per stop,11 12 of them on tyres, three per corner.12 The driver stops on the mark, holds the brake and goes on green; the gunners' buttons give that green.11Six chibi crew per box: four gunners and two jacks. The kart is guided onto its mark, inputs ignored, and a green light releases it.
Stationary times: record 1.80 s, typical about 2.5 s, around 3 s reads as slow.13 The tail is long: a broken jack or stuck nut happens,14 the worst of 2025 was 29.04 s,15 and one 2025 driver's mean was 3.06 s against a median of 2.71 s.16The same shape on a bigger scale: mostly clean, sometimes slow, rarely a disaster. The tail stops at 12 s where F1's reaches 29 s, so one bad roll hurts without ending the race.
Fresh rubber lets a chaser pit first and pass: the undercut. It needs real degradation and an affordable stop.10This is what the five-lap race buys. At three laps it barely exists.
Unsafe release is punished: a team must not send a car into another's path.17Left out of v1. Holding green for traffic depends on other karts, which the browser's prediction cannot see in time.

The sim's handling has no lateral grip today: a kart goes where it points unless it is drifting. A lower steering rate gives understeer and wash-out gives the slide, so "harder to control" is a real change and not only a slower kart. All of it is deterministic, with no random twitches. Mini-turbo charge times do not change.

A straight-line speed cap is a game simplification. The F1 sources describe tyre loss through grip and lap time, not a lower top speed. I kept the cap because you asked for it and players can read it on the speed readout.

The pit lane

Rules, in the order a kart meets them

  1. Choosing. The entry is a visible fork: take it or stay on the track. Steering is the whole control, and nothing is locked in before the entry line.
  2. Entry line. The limiter takes over, braking the kart to 54 km/h within about 17 m and ending any boost. The limit beats coins, the slipstream, a star and every boost.
  3. In the lane. The lane is safe ground. No item can be fired, hazards cannot land, a homing projectile gives up, a star cannot spin anyone out, and tyres wear only by rolling.
  4. Own pit box. Each slot has one, in front of its door in the pit building. Reaching it guides the kart onto its mark. Inputs are ignored and its body stops colliding, so nobody can shunt it. Each box has its own crew, so karts never queue.
  5. The stop. The crew works for the rolled time. Life returns to 100%. A kart that entered on 97% still gets the full stop.
  6. Green. The light goes green and the driver has control again, with a clear run back into the fast lane.
  7. Exit line. The limiter lets go. A painted line guides the kart back onto the track.

Does it fit the courses?

Probably, without moving scenery. Bell Deer and Goldstone keep 8.5 m clear between the outer wall's face (12.5 m from centre) and the front of their pit buildings (21 m), enough for a 7 m lane: a 3.5 m fast lane and a 3.5 m apron. Their start straights give 100 to 130 m of near-straight road. Monarch has narrower run-off and no pit building yet, so it needs one, and is the hardest of the three. I checked this against the layout numbers, not by building it, so slice 3 starts on Bell Deer to prove it.

An example grid of eight stops

Median 2.8 s, mean 3.3 s, 1 stop in 10 over 5 s. With eight karts stopping once, about one race in seven has a disaster and about half have a botched stop.

How it stays fair and in sync. The time comes from a hash of the race seed, the slot and that kart's stop count, the way the runaway wheel uses the seed. It does not draw from the sim's random stream, because a draw would shift every later item roll, and the browser's single-kart prediction could not make the same draw. Server and browser therefore agree on the exact tick of green, with no correction.

Does it change the race?

  • At 3 laps, managing wins, by 2.6 to 4.9 s over pushing with no stop. Pushing with a stop and pushing without one are within about 2.5 s of each other, so a kart that has been hit a few times, at 6% a spin-out, should think about stopping.
  • At 5 laps, everyone should stop once. A second stop gains a pusher at most about 2.5 s, before the risk of another bad roll. Who pits when, and the luck of the roll, decide places.
  • I would not fix 3 laps by wearing tyres faster. Pushing already reaches the red band on lap 2. Any harsher and tyres are gone before the player has learned the course.
  • The oval never needs a stop, which is why decision 4 gives it no lane. A kart on it ends a 3-lap race on about 41%. A pushing kart in a 5-lap race there reaches red during lap 4 and ends near 0%, with no way to fix it; ADR 0083 leaves that as a race of tyre management until playtests say otherwise.

Bots

Bots wear by the same rules and steer along the lane's path when they pit. Thresholds join BotProfile, so tuning stays in one file.

LevelPits whenOn track
BasicLife is 25% or less at a lane approach with a lap or more to go.Drives as today. Often overstays and lives with it.
ProLife is 30% or less, or would run out with laps to go.Stops optional drifts below 30%.
AceProjects life to the flag, and pits if it would finish under 15% and the stop pays. Never on the last lap unless it is under 10%.Manages when a no-stop race works.

Each bot's threshold varies by a few points from the seed, so bots do not all pit on the same lap. The rubber band (0.92 to 1.08 at Basic) is larger than most of the tyre loss, so it will hide tyres on Basic bots. Whether to narrow the rubber band can wait until we have seen it race.

Decisions to record

  • ADR 0080, tyres wear. Supersedes ADR 0055's consequence that the gauge reads something other than wear, and ADR 0056's gauge holding at 100%. ADR 0055 dropped wear because the gauge needed something to read and wear had never been built, not because wear had failed. Coins and their rule stay as they are.
  • ADR 0081, a pit lane to drive. Supersedes the glossary's "no pit lane to drive", which it credits to ADR 0050.
  • ADR 0082, the stop is rolled from the seed.
  • ADR 0083, the creator picks tyres and distance.
  • Glossary. Add pit lane, pit box, pit limiter, pit crew, stop and tyre life. "Tyre wear" calls the percentage how worn the tyres are, but 100% means new, so it should read as life left.

Tests, all through step and the fake transport

  • A kart that drifts a lap ends it on less life than one that lifts, and a spin-out takes the tuned amount.
  • A worn kart reaches a lower top speed and turns less than a fresh one on the same inputs.
  • A kart in the lane never exceeds the limit, whatever boost it brought in.
  • A lap through the pits counts, and the kart keeps its place by spline progress.
  • A stop restores 100%, its length repeats for the same seed, and 10,000 seeds match the chance table.
  • Prediction and server agree on the tick of green under a 100 ms round trip.
  • Every bot finishes on every course and level, tyres on.

Risks and what is left out

Risks

  • Pile-on for the kart behind. A hit already costs 1 s and three coins, and now 6% life too. Trailing karts could fall further behind. The 0% floor and the free stop limit it, but watch it in playtests.
  • Keyboards. Steering is all or nothing on keys, so wash-out and reduced steering may feel like a broken kart rather than a worn one. Slice 2's feel test decides this before anything is built on it.
  • The numbers are starting points. Wear comes from bots on today's handling, lap costs from a model. Neither has been raced. Expect to change them in slice 5.
  • Monarch. Its narrow run-off and lack of a pit building make it the costliest lane. If it proves too hard it can ship without one, as the oval does.
  • Griefing in the lane. A kart can park in the fast lane. Contact is harmless at that speed, and the race's end cap limits how long it can matter.

Left out of v1

  • Compounds (soft, medium, hard). The most obvious next step, but pointless until races are 5 laps and strategy exists.
  • Reversible tyre heat, graining and blistering. More true to F1, but it needs another readout.
  • Separate wear per tyre. ADR 0031 allows for it. One number is easier to read at speed.
  • Punctures and unsafe release penalties. Punctures feel unfair in a race this short; the release hold breaks prediction.
  • Dirty air wearing a following kart. A neat counter to the slipstream for later.
  • Team-mate double stacking, since each kart has its own box and crew.

Method and sources

Measured: eight bots, eight seeds, three levels and four courses through today's sim, 768 kart-races from a throwaway crate outside the repo. Modelled: lap cost, pit cost and strategy tables. Chosen by judgement: every wear rate, effect factor and roll chance, tuned so the results above come out where the design wants them. The rates for walls and kerbs and the formula for bumps have not been checked against any data.
