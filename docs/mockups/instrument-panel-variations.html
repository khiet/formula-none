Instrument panel: five treatments

Same mock Race View, same live readouts (position, lap, lap time, best lap, item slot, speed), only the top-left changes. The course map stays bottom-right as today. Each frame scales with its width, so widen or narrow the window to see how each holds up.

1. SNES Super Mario Kart pixel type, outlined gold, lap flags, bevelled item box

Every readout is text with a hard brown outline, no dark box behind it. Laps are counted with checkered flags you fill in as you cross the line. The position is a huge numeral. Time uses the 0'27"30 SNES notation. Scanlines are on this frame only, to sell the look; they would be optional.

3. Arcade neon skewed, glowing, speed bar, OutRun and Daytona lineage

Loud by design. Everything leans forward, the position glows magenta, laps and speed glow cyan, the item slot is a yellow neon frame. Speed gets a bar because arcade racers make speed the hero. Risk: the glow eats contrast over bright sky and the skew costs some legibility.

4. Minimal thin type, no boxes, one hairline, Art of Rally quiet

Nothing but type on the View, held together by a soft shadow. One giant light-weight position numeral, lap and times at a glance, a hairline, then speed and the item slot in a quiet footer. Lets the track breathe. Risk: thin strokes over a busy background need the shadow to carry them.

5. Pit board hand-slotted plates hung from the top edge, motorsport props

A pit crew's board held over the wall: each digit is its own slotted plate, a little crooked, yellow plates for the position. Rows read P3, LAP 2/3, the running lap time and best. Speed and item sit as small tags underneath. Playful and very on-theme for a game called Formula None; the live-ticking time is the one thing a real board could never do.
