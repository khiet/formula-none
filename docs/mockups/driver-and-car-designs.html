Mockup / driver and car

Three ways to build the driver and the car

Each direction swaps the Kenney kart for an open-wheel car with a halo, wings and sidepods, and gives the driver a human face for the portrait. Pick a livery to see all three in it.

Livery

A. Chibi F1

kart proportions / big head / soft tyres

An F1 car squashed to the kart's collision box: short wheelbase, oversized wheels and helmet, so it still reads as a toy at chase-cam distance. The face is cartoon-human: big eyes, soft shading, warm skin.

  • ModelFits today's collision box, so no sim or tuning change. About 2k triangles; one new Blender build, not Kenney.
  • LiveryThree paints (primary, secondary, accent) plus helmet. The glossary's one "fixed colour" becomes a palette: an ADR.
  • FaceOnly in the portrait; in the car the visor is down. Eight portraits drawn as SVG, one per livery slot or picked by the player.

B. Grounded

real proportions / slim nose / medium tyres

Close to a modern F1 car: long wheelbase, airbox over the driver's head, halo, deep sidepods. The face is the most realistic of the three: smaller eyes, anatomical jaw, stubble and shading.

  • ModelLonger than today's box. Either the sim's kart dimensions grow (bumps, items, every replay fixture re-recorded) or the car overhangs it.
  • LiverySame three-paint split as A; more surface for stripes, so it reads best up close and in the Results Scene.
  • FaceRealistic faces sit uneasily next to Kenney's flat-shaded tracks. Hardest to draw eight of that hold together.

C. Low-poly

faceted / flat materials / hard tyres / recommended

Grounded's silhouette built from flat-shaded facets, the same language as the Kenney tracks. The face is a faceted bust: planes for brow, cheek, nose and jaw give it bone structure without needing a texture.

  • ModelBuilt in Blender from primitives in a build.py like today's kart; around 1k triangles, flat materials, no textures.
  • LiveryPrimary, secondary, accent and helmet as four named materials the View recolours, the way it recolours Livery now.
  • FaceThe bust can be a real mesh too, so the same head sits in the cockpit with the visor up on the podium.

Liveries borrow each team's colours only: no team names, crests, sponsors or real race numbers, and the drivers are made-up faces, not likenesses of Hamilton or Verstappen. Shipping real marks or real drivers' faces needs licences from the teams and the drivers.
