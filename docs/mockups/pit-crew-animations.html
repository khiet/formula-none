Mockup / pit box crew

Pit crew animations

Three ways to act a stop out at the pit box: who stands where, who crouches, who lifts the kart, who works the nuts and who holds the sign that says go. Every crew member is a chibi with the driver's own head kit, so the crew reads as people and no two faces match. The stages play the stop the sim rolls: pick a tier and the crew acts that roll out past a clean stop's time, as ADR 0082 has it.

Recommendation

Ship version B, the lollipop crew of seven, with the head kit of this page on the driver too. It is the classic picture of a stop that every player recognises: a jack at each end, four gunners crouched at the wheels and one person standing in front of the kart holding a round sign that spins from STOP to GO and lifts as the kart is let go. It is the smallest crew that shows every job the brief asks for, and it is the one where the sign is the thing the driver looks at, which is the sign's point.

  1. A, the cap crew of five, is the cheap fallback if forty figures a lane prove too many parts: each gunner does both wheels on a side, which is a clear, busy act, but at a clean stop's 2.65 s the gunners sprint and the stop reads rushed.
  2. C, the helmeted crew of nine with wheel carriers and a light paddle, is the most faithful to a modern stop, but the helmets hide the hair, which is half of what tells the faces apart, and the extra bodies crowd the chase camera's frame.
  3. In every version the sim's clock is the word on green: the act is cut to the rolled time, the sign and the gantry light flip on the tick of green, and nothing drawn hurries or holds the kart.

The head kit every version shares

The driver's head from assets/karts/build.py, rebuilt in code: an eight-sided ball with a short jaw and a flat forward face, big eyes, ears, one of the hair styles and facial hairs in livery.ts, earrings. The crew adds what the driver's head has not got yet: brows at an angle, a nose, a mouth, glasses, four more hair styles and two more skin tones, and a little height. A cast of ten per pit box is drawn from a seed of the slot, with no hair style used twice, and each version takes its crew from the same cast, so the front jack is the same person in all three. The driver sits first, with the same kit, to show the two read as one cast.

Head
Radius 0.3 m for the driver and 0.26 m for the crew; jaw 0.86 wide and 0.78 high below the middle; the eyes on the forward flat, whites 0.1 by 0.13 m with irises proud of them.
Varies
Skin from five tones; hair from eleven styles in nine colours; facial hair from five kinds and a moustache; brow angle and weight; eye spacing and size; nose size; a line, a smile, a grin or an open mouth; glasses; earrings; height 0.92 to 1.08 of the base.
Costs
A head is 15 to 30 flat-shaded parts. Five to nine faces per box over eight boxes is 40 to 72 heads, each built once; only the pose changes per frame.
Don't
Don't hide the face under a visor or a balaclava in the versions that keep the hair: the face is the point of the kit. Don't name a crew member anywhere; like the driver's name, the cast shows nowhere.

Three versions of the stop

All three run the same clock. The kart rolls onto its mark, the stop counts up on the Instrument panel, and at the rolled time the light goes green. Past 3 s, a clean stop's longest, the crew acts the roll out. The chase camera is the player's own view, 6 m behind and 3.2 m up; the other cameras are for judging the poses.

A. The cap crew

Five crew, caps, two gunners who each do a side
On the mark0.0 s

The small crew. A jack at each end, a gunner on each side who works the front wheel then runs to the rear, and a chief who stands ahead of the nose with a two-faced board held over their head, STOP toward the driver, swung to GO on the tick of green. Fresh wheels wait on the apron beside each corner before the kart arrives, so the gunner reaches for the new wheel without a carrier. Everyone wears a cap in the slot's key colour, peak forward, with the hair showing under it.

Crouching and standing: the gunners crouch at each wheel and sprint upright between them; the jacks stand, leaning into the lever as they pump; the chief stands with both arms up. On a slow roll the left gunner goes back down at the rear left; on a botched roll the right gunner runs over to help and the chief comes to point; on a disaster the front jack lets go, the kart drops on its nose, the front jack steps back with arms up and the gunners stand off until the jack is pumped again.

For the game: 5 figures by 8 boxes is 40 figures, the cheapest of the three, and the busiest act per person. Against: a clean stop gives each wheel 1.05 s, so the gunners' sprint reads frantic, and two gunners cannot show a corner going wrong while the rest stand ready, which is what the slow and botched tiers mean.

Don't stage the fresh wheels on the lane side of the mark, where a passing kart would clip them: the sim has no wheel there, and the mockup keeps them on the apron.

B. The lollipop crew

Seven crew, headsets, a gunner at every wheel and a lollipop
On the mark0.0 s

The classic stop. A jack at each end, a gunner crouched at every wheel, and the lollipop: a round sign on a pole, held by a seventh crew member who stands off the nose on the building's side, right in the driver's eyeline. It shows STOP in poster red through the stop, spins to GO in go green half a second before the tick, and lifts clear as the kart is let go, the holder stepping aside. Everyone wears a headset over their hair, band over the crown and a mic to the mouth, which keeps every face and every haircut in view.

Each gunner's act is one wheel: judder the nut off, pull the worn wheel out and shove it back behind them, pull the fresh wheel in from the apron, judder the nut on, and throw a hand up. The jack crew pump the lever twice to lift, drop it on the last beat and drag the jack clear. The acts of the roll are the ones the View already has: on a slow roll the rear-left gunner goes back down alone; on a botched roll the other three run over and crowd that corner; on a disaster the front jack lets go and everyone stands off until it is pumped again.

For the game: 7 by 8 is 56 figures, every job in the brief has a body, and the sign is held where the chase camera sees it over the kart's head. Against: two more figures than the current six, and the lollipop holder is in the kart's path, so the step aside must land before the kart moves.

Don't let the lollipop lift before the tick: the sim rolls the stop, and a sign that lifts early tells the player a lie the panel's clock then contradicts.

C. The helmet crew

Nine crew, open-face helmets, wheel carriers and a light paddle
On the mark0.0 s

The modern stop. Jacks at both ends, four gunners, a wheel carrier on each side standing between the two wheels with a fresh wheel held at the chest, who crouches to pull the worn wheel and push the fresh one on while the gunner keeps the gun on the nut, and a chief at the gantry with a handheld light paddle: a red lamp through the stop, green on the tick, raised overhead. Everyone wears an open-face helmet with the visor up in the slot's key colour: skin, brows, beards, mouths and glasses still tell them apart, but the hair is gone.

Crouching and standing: four gunners crouch for the whole stop, the carriers stand and crouch in turn, the jacks and the chief stand. On a slow roll the rear-left gunner goes back down and the left carrier waits beside them; on a botched roll both carriers and the far gunners run to that corner; on a disaster the front jack lets go and the crew stands off.

For the game: the most faithful stop, and the carriers make the wheel change itself legible from the chase camera, where a gunner's hands are hidden behind the kart. Against: 9 by 8 is 72 figures, the most parts of the three; the helmets hide half the face kit; nine bodies round a 1.8 m kart crowd the frame, and the chief's paddle is off to one side, where the player's eye is not.

Don't give the paddle's lamp its own clock: it is the gantry light in the hand, and reads the same stop.

How it fits the code

  • apps/web/src/pit-box-meshes.ts keeps its shape: crewWork reads the kart readout's stop, and stance(member, work) grows into stance(member, work, secondsOnMark), a pure function of the member, the work and the stop clock that returns a place, a yaw, a pose name and a pose blend. The page's choreography is that function, written for three rosters.
  • The head kit moves to a module the kart model and the crew share: head-kit.ts builds a head from a face record the way build.py does, so the driver and the crew come from one builder. The driver's record gains brows, a nose and a mouth in the same change, or stays as it is; either way the parts are optional.
  • Figures stay instanced: one InstancedMesh per part kind (torso, upper arm, forearm, thigh, shin, boot, hand, head shell, eye, hair cap and so on), posed from a rig that writes one matrix per part per figure. A crew member is about 30 parts; version B is 56 figures and about 1,700 instances per lane, drawn only within PIT_DRAW_RANGE_M.
  • The signs are two CanvasTextures in the Kenney pixel face, STOP and GO, built once; the gantry light's materials swap as today.
  • Nothing here enters the sim. A test through the public seam drives stance with a stop readout and asserts the pose at the tick of green and the step aside before the kart's first metre.
  • The style guide's pit box entry gains the crew's heights, the kit's parts and the sign's two faces in the same PR.
