Suzuqa Patch, trackside objects

The course is a road through empty grass. Twelve pieces of scenery to fill the green space and give each corner a landmark: the Circuit Wheel by the first corner, woods along the back section, a pit building the grid faces, and a few jokes. Every one is scenery in the glTF and nothing collides with it, so the sim, the sidecar and the replay fixtures do not change; the four that move are moved by the View from the clock, as the marshal's flags are. The map is laid live from the same turtle steps as trackgen/layouts/brands_patch.py, and each footprint's clearance from the nearest wall is computed on it.

Clearance is grass between the object's footprint and the nearest wall, measured on the map's centreline samples (walls stand 12 m off it). A low object keeps 3 m; anything over 3 m tall keeps 8 m, so it never comes between the chase camera and a kart on the outside of a corner. Hover a row or a pin to find it.

Objects

Each card is one family for trackgen: a drawing in the style guide's manner, where it stands, what it is built from, what it costs and why it is there. Red-topped cards stand still; gold-topped ones move in the View.

Placing rules

Where a thing may stand

  • Beyond the walls, always. Nothing on the asphalt, the run-off or the walls' line; the sim never learns a prop exists, so a prop a kart could reach would be a wall it cannot see.
  • Three metres of grass from the wall for anything low, eight for anything tall (over 3 m). The chase camera hangs behind and above the kart; on the outside of a corner it can sit over the wall, and a tall prop there would stand between it and the kart.
  • Seen down a straight. A landmark earns its faces by being visible for seconds, not frames: the wheel sits ahead of the start straight, the bottle at the end of 130R's run, the clock at the lap's last look, the airship over everything.
  • The bridge keeps its sky. Nothing taller than the deck's parapet within 60 m of the crossing, so the deck still reads as a bridge from the underpass.
  • Woods between legs, not on them. A wood between the back straight and 130R hides the next leg, so the figure eight reads as roads through a landscape rather than a drawing on a lawn. Trees keep 5 m apart and 3 m from the wall.

What a thing may not be

  • Not mistakable for a kart, an item box or a hazard. No kart-sized box in a livery colour near the road, no gold cube, no lone cone, no stack of tyres off the course. The sheep stay fenced so none reads as an obstacle the sim does not know.
  • No numbers, crests or team names anywhere (ADR 0035): the pit garages are colours only.
  • No real sponsor, no real circuit's mark (ADR 0007, ADR 0038): lettering comes from sponsors.py and nowhere else.
  • No face the player must read. Oyaji is the marshal and stays the only character; the fair is his tent, not him twice.
  • Nothing that reads the race. A scoreboard or a camera that follows the leader needs the standings in the View; that is a later decision, not scenery.

Build notes

The layout

Decor gains two lists. A Prop is a kind and a spot, either hung on the lap (s and an offset left of the centreline, as every placement is today) or set in the world (x, y) for the infield, with a heading. A Wood is a kind of tree, a patch (a disc or a line), a count and a seed; the builder scatters the trees itself and rejects any within 3 m of a wall, 5 m of a neighbour or inside a prop's footprint, so a layout never lists a tree.

decor=Decor(
    ...,
    props=[
        Prop("circuit-wheel", Spot(s=86.0, offset=34.0)),
        Prop("arch", Spot(s=path.segment("dunlop").s_mid)),
        Prop("pits", Spot(s=0.0, offset=-26.0), length=90.0),
        Prop("airship", World(-60.0, -40.0), radius=60.0, height=45.0),
        Prop("pond", World(-330.0, 80.0)),
        Prop("paddock", World(-350.0, 86.0)),
        Prop("fair", World(-295.0, 152.0), heading=-50.0),
        Prop("bottle", World(-120.0, 72.0)),
        Prop("camera-crane", World(-148.0, 6.0), heading=100.0),
        Prop("clock-tower", World(-40.0, 80.0), heading=-110.0),
    ],
    woods=[
        Wood("pine", Disc(-215.0, -70.0, 35.0), count=40, seed=1),
        Wood("round", Disc(-150.0, 5.0, 12.0), count=15, seed=2),
        Wood("round", Disc(-300.0, 72.0, 14.0), count=12, seed=3),
        Wood("pine", Disc(-230.0, 190.0, 25.0), count=25, seed=4),
        Wood("pine", Disc(-400.0, 100.0, 25.0), count=30, seed=5),
        Wood("round", Disc(-60.0, -45.0, 20.0), count=15, seed=6),
        Wood("cherry", Line((-5.0, -150.0), (-105.0, -130.0)), count=12, seed=7),
    ],
)

Each kind is a builder in a new trackgen/props.py, one function per family returning scenery meshes through ByMaterial, so layout.py does not grow by a thousand lines. decorate appends them after the bridges. The track tests hold every prop to its clearance rule and every wood's trees to theirs, the way test_stands_and_gantry.py holds the stands.

Moving props and the View

Today every scenery mesh is merged per material with its origin at the world's zero. A moving prop needs a node whose origin is its pivot: the wheel's hub, the airship's nose, a sheep's feet, the clock's spindle. SceneryMesh gains an optional pivot; build.py sets the object's origin there and names the node Prop.<kind>.<n>.<part>.

A scenery-motion.ts in the web app (Presentation) finds those nodes by name after the track loads and advances them from performance.now(): the rim turns and each gondola is counter-rotated to hang level, the airship rides its circle, the sheep walk a seeded wander inside the paddock, the clock's hands read the viewer's clock four minutes behind. Nothing in it reads the bridge or the sim, so a stalled client's wheel simply stops.

Palette

MATERIALS gains the trees (canopy-pine, canopy, blossom, each with a -shade, and trunk), water, wool and wool-shade, cola-red and cola-red-shade, and a hull for the airship. Garage doors, gondolas and flags reuse crowd-<slot>, the eight key colours. Everything else is already there: carbon, the stand greys, kerb red, the inks.

What else changes

  • A card per family in docs/style-guide.html, in the same PR as the family.
  • assets/LICENSES.md is unchanged: all of it is built from primitives here, CC0.
  • A re-export of brands-patch.glb per slice. The sidecar diff is empty every time; a test asserts it.

Issues

Props in the layout, and the woods

Decor.props and Decor.woods, trackgen/props.py, the three trees and the cherry row with their scatter and the clearance test, the four canopy materials, the style guide's Woods card, the re-export. No moving part. The largest visible change for the least code.

The Circuit Wheel

The family, the pivot on SceneryMesh and the named nodes in build.py, scenery-motion.ts with the rim and the sixteen gondolas, the asset card. Sets the pattern every later moving prop follows.

The arch and the pit building

Two static landmarks with lettering through TextProp, the way the hoardings letter today. The pit building's doors in grid order, the flags, the cards.

The airship and the clock

Two more nodes on the motion module: the airship's circle and heading, the clock's two hands. The clock's four-minute lateness is a constant in scenery-motion.ts, commented as the joke it is.

The hairpin fair, the pond, the bottle and the crane

Static jokes in one PR: four families, four cards, one re-export.

The sheep

Six nodes, the paddock fence, the seeded wander that never leaves the fence, and the test that holds the paddock clear of the wall by the full wander radius.

Out of scope

  • Collisions with any prop. If a prop ever needs to stop a kart, it becomes a tyre wall or a wall in the sidecar, with its own ADR.
  • Lighting, shadows or a time of day. The View's sun does not move for a Ferris wheel.
  • Props on the oval.
  • Anything that reads the race: a scoreboard, a camera crane that follows the leader, a crowd that cheers a pass.
  • Sound. The airship is silent, the fountain is silent, the sheep are silent.
  • The course map, the Instrument panel and the Title and Results Scenes.
