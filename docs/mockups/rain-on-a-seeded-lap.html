Issue 332 / card X6 / design only

Rain on a seeded lap

Three versions of rain, measured with bots where a version touches the sim. Decided: grip only, at the mild level (ADR 0103). It changes the race on every course and pick, and the pit lane already responds to it without a second tyre.

Findings

Rain already sends karts to the pit lane

Grip alone raises the stops made after the rain arrives. Over five laps, Ace bots go from stops a kart after rain in the dry control to at the mild level. Nothing about the pit call changed: the same wear costs more laps of slower driving.

A wet tyre would rarely be a choice

Packs already stop times a kart over three laps in the dry on courses with a lane. Most karts would collect a wet tyre at a stop they were making anyway. The mild level costs about 2.9 s a lap on new tyres and a stop about 8 s (ADR 0081), so two wet laps do not repay an extra stop.

Heavy grip loss overruns the end cap

Over five laps, karts that miss the end cap go from of 512 in the dry control to at mild and at heavy. Bots stay on the road at both levels: no cell put more than 2 of 64 karts on grass.

Dropping rain stays open. The case for it is pi's: fixed atmosphere is cheaper, and ADRs 0085 and 0095 are still recorded as pending the maintainer's playtest. Whichever version is chosen, the build should wait for that playtest, since B and C both sit on top of the tyre tune.

How it reads in a pack

The sky, road and streaks are the same in all three versions. The open question is spray: enough to say the road is wet, without hiding karts, item boxes or hazards. Drag the wetness and compare the spray levels.

Proposed rules for the look

  • Spray budget: a low plume at each kart's rear wheels, no higher than the top of the wheels and about two kart lengths long. Rear wings, helmets and liveries stay visible through a pack.
  • Always on top: item boxes, the runaway wheel, drones and worn-tyre smoke stay readable. Spray is pale and low, smoke dark and rising.
  • Road: darker asphalt and a soft sheen under each kart. Kerbs, white lines and boost pads keep their colours.
  • Sky: grey with the sun's light scaled down, using the View's existing light scale (ADR 0098). No lightning or flashes.
  • Roofs: streaks stop under Monarch's tunnel and Goldstone's hangar through presentation zones. Grip stays wet there, since the sim never reads a zone.
  • Less motion: a viewer who asks for less motion gets fewer streaks, as the fireworks do.
  • Sound: a rain bed under the SFX toggle, levelled by wetness and never pitched.

When it arrives

The seed picks the lap from a hash, drawing nothing from the sim's random stream, as a stop's roll does (ADR 0082). Rain comes on lap 2 or later, so the field has spread, and up to the lap before the last, except that a Sprint can also take its last lap, since it has only two to choose from.

  • Trigger: the first tick any kart starts the picked lap, so the whole field gets wet together. Every screen sees one sky.
  • Warning: the clouds gather over the leader's last quarter of the lap before. They only change the look.
  • Ramp: wetness climbs from 0 to 1 over 20 s, then holds to the flag. It never dries; a second change is a later decision.
  • Network: the server's state holds the tick the rain began and the snapshot carries it, as the prediction takes other race facts from the snapshot. A snapshot's 100 ms on a 20 s ramp is 0.5% of the wet, too little to feel.
  • The pick: Weather, Dry or Rain, chosen by the creator with the other picks (ADR 0083). A kept room or replay with no pick is Dry and hashes as it does today. It starts on Dry until it has been played.

The grip

Wetness scales one extra row on top of the tyre tables. Top speed is untouched, so the speedo and the gauge keep meaning what they mean today, and the loss shows in the corners. These are trial numbers, a starting tune.

New tyres slide a little in the wet, and worn tyres slide most. At the mild level a kart at 0% life can slide 0.20 rad, under the drift's 0.30. Bots keep today's rules, including the pit call; the Ace's table of lap costs stays dry, so it will underrate a stop in the wet.

The trial

A throwaway patch multiplied the grip factors by the table above, with no change to bots, wear or the pit call. Packs were eight bots on Tyres On, 8 seeds a cell, with rain on lap 2 of 3 or lap 3 of 5, at full strength with no ramp. The lone laps held an Ace bot at a set life with the wet on from the start, over 4 seeds. It is about 400 races, enough to compare the levels and too few to tune: Bell Deer's mild lap coming out faster than dry is noise, or the slower steering smoothing the bot's line as ADR 0087 saw.

What the trial did not measure

  • Whether bots miss the pit lane fork. A slide of 0.15 rad or more has carried them past it before (ADR 0087), and worn wet tyres now reach that. The build's sweep must count missed lanes.
  • Tyres Off packs, the 20 s ramp, and any kart on wets: version C's numbers are arithmetic, not races.
  • How it feels on a keyboard, where steering is all or nothing. Only a playtest answers that.
