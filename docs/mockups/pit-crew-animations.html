Pit crew animation: the lollipop crew

The stop acted out at the pit box by a crew of seven: a jack at each end of the kart, a gunner crouched at every wheel and a lollipop held in the driver's eyeline that says STOP until the tick of green. Every crew member is a chibi with the driver's own head kit, so the crew reads as people and no two faces match. The stage plays the stop the sim rolls: pick a tier and the crew acts that roll out past a clean stop's time, as ADR 0082 has it. Every beat of the stop has a sound; turn them on in the picker.

The decision

The lollipop crew of seven, with the head kit on the driver too. It is the classic picture of a stop that every player recognises: a jack at each end, four gunners crouched at the wheels and one person standing off the nose holding a round sign that spins from STOP to GO and lifts as the kart is let go. It is the smallest crew that gives every job a body, and the sign is the thing the driver looks at, which is the sign's point. The earlier takes, a cap crew of five with sprinting gunners and a helmeted crew of nine with wheel carriers, were set aside: the first reads rushed at a clean stop and the second hides the hair under helmets and crowds the chase camera.

  • The sim's clock is the word on green: the act is cut to the rolled time, the sign and the gantry light flip on the tick, and nothing drawn hurries or holds the kart.
  • Everyone wears a headset over their hair, band over the crown and a mic to the mouth, which keeps every face and every haircut in view.
  • Each beat of the stop fires one cue, from the same clock the poses read, so a sound never plays for a movement the player cannot see.

The head kit

The driver's head from assets/karts/build.py, rebuilt in code: an eight-sided ball with a short jaw and a flat forward face, big eyes, ears, one of the hair styles and facial hairs in livery.ts, earrings. The crew adds what the driver's head has not got yet: brows at an angle, a nose, a mouth, glasses, four more hair styles and two more skin tones, and a little height. The seven are drawn from a seed of the slot, with no hair style used twice. The driver sits first, with the same kit, to show the two read as one cast.

Head
Radius 0.3 m for the driver and 0.26 m for the crew; jaw 0.86 wide and 0.78 high below the middle; the eyes on the forward flat, whites 0.1 by 0.13 m with irises proud of them.
Varies
Skin from five tones; hair from eleven styles in nine colours; facial hair from five kinds and a moustache; brow angle and weight; eye spacing and size; nose size; a line, a smile, a grin or an open mouth; glasses; earrings; height 0.92 to 1.08 of the base.
Headset
A half-torus band of 0.335 radius over the crown, cups of 0.09 radius at the ears, a mic boom to the right corner of the mouth, all carbon. It sits over any hair style without hiding it.
Costs
A head is 15 to 30 flat-shaded parts. Seven faces per box over eight boxes is 56 heads, each built once; only the pose changes per frame.
Don't
Don't hide the face under a visor or a balaclava: the face is the point of the kit. Don't name a crew member anywhere; like the driver's name, the cast shows nowhere.

The stop

The kart rolls onto its mark on the guide, the stop counts up on the Instrument panel, and at the rolled time the light goes green. Past 3 s, a clean stop's longest, the crew acts the roll out. The chase camera is the player's own view, 6 m behind and 3.2 m up; the other cameras are for judging the poses.

The lollipop crew

Seven crew, headsets, a gunner at every wheel and a lollipop
On the mark0.0 s

Before the kart. The crew waits in a row on the apron and runs out on the box call. The gunners crouch at their hubs with the gun up, each gun on an air line that hangs from the boom over the box. The front jack is walked in nose first on the guide. The rear jack waits on the building's side and swings in behind the kart as its tail clears the mark, because the kart drives through where it has to stand.

On the mark. The jack crew pump the lever twice and the kart comes up 0.16 m at each end, the front a beat ahead of the rear. Each gunner's act is one wheel: judder the nut off, pull the worn wheel out and shove it back behind them so it rolls clear, pull the fresh wheel in from the apron, judder the nut on, and throw a hand up. The driver's eyes are on the lollipop the whole time; the holder's are on the driver, with one look down the lane for traffic a second before the tick.

The release. The sign spins to GO half a second before the tick, the jacks drop the kart on the last beat and are dragged clear, and on the tick the lollipop lifts and the holder steps aside out of the kart's path. The gantry light goes green for the hold and the kart leaves under its own power. The crew walks back to the row.

The acts of the roll are the ones the View already has: on a slow roll the rear-left gunner goes back down alone; on a botched roll the other three run over and crowd that corner; on a disaster the front jack lets go, the kart drops on its nose, the crew stands off, and the front wheels are done again once the jack is pumped back up.

For the game: 7 by 8 is 56 figures, every job has a body, and the sign is held where the chase camera sees it over the kart's head. Against: one more figure than the current six, and the holder is in the kart's path, so the step aside must land before the kart moves.

Don't let the lollipop lift before the tick: the sim rolls the stop, and a sign that lifts early tells the player a lie the panel's clock then contradicts. Don't stage the fresh wheels on the lane side of the mark where a passing kart would clip them; they wait on the apron side of each corner.

Places
Front jack at the nose, z +2.35, rear jack at the tail, z −2.35; gunners at x ±1.1 beside each hub, z ±0.52; the lollipop holder at (0.75, 2.9) facing the kart, stepping to (1.35, 2.9) at the tick; the row on the apron at x 2.6, 0.7 m apart. The pit box is 2.2 by 2.8 m with the gantry at its front corner on the building's side.
Beats
At a clean 2.65 s: jack 0 to 0.4, nuts off 0.25 to 0.85, wheels off 0.85 to 1.25, wheels on 1.25 to 1.65, nuts on 1.65 to 2.15, hands up at 2.15, drop from T−0.3 to T−0.05. A record stop scales the beats by T/2.65; a longer one holds the hands up until 3 s, then acts the roll.
Sign
A 0.34 m disc on a 1.3 m pole, STOP in white on poster red toward the driver, GO in black on go green on the back. It spins over 0.4 s ending 0.2 s before the tick, lifts 0.7 m over 0.3 s from the tick, and is never shown GO toward the lane while the holder turns.
Jacks
A 0.34 by 0.5 m trolley on two wheels with a 1 m lever. The front jack rolls in over the last 0.7 s of the guide; the rear swings in from (1.0, −2.0) in the 0.3 s around the mark. The lever is pumped to lift, flipped up to drop, and the jack is dragged clear over the 0.55 s around the tick.
Air lines
A boom at 4.8 m from the building's face across the box, above the chase camera's frame once the kart is on its mark; a reel over each side at x ±1.2 and a line sagging to each gun's hand.
Acts
Slow 3.8 s: the rear-left nut again from 3.0 until T−0.5. Botched 6.0 s: the rear-left nut stuck from 3.0, the other three run over at 3.25, free at T−0.5. Disaster 10.5 s: the front jack fails at 3.0, the nose drops, the crew stands off, the jack is pumped back at T−3.0 and the front wheels are redone from T−2.5 to T−1.1: nuts off for 0.5 s, the wheel pulled and pushed back, nuts on.

The sounds of the stop

One cue per beat, played from the loop above when sound is on and from the buttons here. Each is synthesised with the DSP kit of the sound effects page, which is assets/audio/build.py's kit line for line, so a cue that reads well here ports to a WAV by the same recipe. Three of them ship already: the box chime, the wheel gun and the jack drop. The left and right gunners play their cues a few hundredths apart, panned to their side, so four guns read as a crew and not a loop.

AtBeatSoundRecipe
Levels
Every render is tamed to a 0.95 peak as build.py does, and none is raised to it. The stage plays the gunners' cues at 0.8 of the jacks' and the launch stand-in at 0.6, so the sign and the jacks lead the mix. The shipped wheel gun renders at a 0.13 peak, under every other cue, so the page plays it at 2.5 times; the game should raise its gain in build.py or in the mixer in the same change.
Panning
Lane side left, building side right, half way: the chase camera looks up the lane with the building on the right, so a gun on the right of the frame sounds on the right.
Not here
The engine's rev and the tyres' chirp as the kart leaves belong to the engine loop of ADR 0025; the launch cue here is a stand-in to hear the release whole and does not go to build.py. No voice: the crew does not speak, like the driver.
Don't
Don't fire a cue from the stop's tier: the botched and disaster sounds come from the act's beats, the same ones the poses read, so a stall never sounds while a gunner's hand is up.

How it fits the code

  • apps/web/src/pit-box-meshes.ts keeps its shape: crewWork reads the kart readout's stop, and stance(member, work) grows into stance(member, work, secondsOnMark), a pure function of the member, the work and the stop clock that returns a place, a yaw, a pose name and a pose blend. The page's choreography is that function, written for a roster of seven.
  • The beats are one table, STOP_BEATS, that both the stance and the cues read: a beat's start on the stop clock, scaled by the stationary time below a clean stop's. soundCues(before, after) in audio-cues.ts fires a cue when the stop's stationary_so_far_s crosses a beat's start, as it fires the wheel gun on the mark today, and the tier's act beats come from the same table.
  • The head kit moves to a module the kart model and the crew share: head-kit.ts builds a head from a face record the way build.py does, so the driver and the crew come from one builder. The driver's record gains brows, a nose and a mouth in the same change, or stays as it is; either way the parts are optional.
  • Figures stay instanced: one InstancedMesh per part kind (torso, upper arm, forearm, thigh, shin, boot, hand, head shell, eye, hair cap and so on), posed from a rig that writes one matrix per part per figure. A crew member is about 30 parts; 56 figures is about 1,700 instances per lane, drawn only within PIT_DRAW_RANGE_M. The air lines are one LineSegments per lane.
  • The signs are two CanvasTextures in the Kenney pixel face, STOP and GO, built once; the gantry light's materials swap as today.
  • The new cues go into build.py as jack_lift, wheel_off, wheel_on, gun_stall, jack_fail, lollipop_spin, release_chime and boots, with the shorter wheel gun as a bursts argument, and into the manifest in audio.ts; the preload check in the e2e counts them.
  • Nothing here enters the sim. A test through the public seam drives stance and soundCues with a stop readout and asserts the pose and the cue at the tick of green, and the step aside before the kart's first metre.
  • The style guide's pit box entry gains the crew's heights, the kit's parts, the sign's two faces and the boom in the same PR.
